Sound coverage study guide

1-7
Recommended workflow

Set the project basis first, calibrate the plan, define each receiver requirement, then configure and place sources. Check both area compliance and loudspeaker equipment qualification before printing the study.

  1. Set the study basis.Choose paging, public address, or siren. Enter the receiver-plane width, depth, height, and calculation-grid spacing.
  2. Import and calibrate the plan.Load the background, enter its drawing scale and raster DPI, then select Apply scale. Use Measure between two known points to verify the result. Width and depth describe the study area; drawing scale converts the imported image into real metres.
  3. Define receiver requirements.Set project ambient, required margin, coverage floor, and assessment maximum. Add noise zones for areas with different ambient levels; drag their handles to resize them.
  4. Enter each source datum.Select a source and copy an approved datasheet test point into Manufacturer acoustic datum. Then set its operating tap, rated power, class, mounting height, aim, directivity, and verified losses.
  5. Place and aim sources.Place devices individually or use Auto-place and draw an area. Auto-place calculates a screening layout from the active criteria and sets every new source to 0 degrees. Drag the circular handle to rotate a source.
  6. Select and batch edit.Press S, then drag over sources or draw a box on empty space. Drag one selected source to move the group. Use the inspector to change azimuth, power, class, or other shared values, then select Apply changes.
  7. Review and issue.Switch between Compliance, Sound level, and S/N margin. Verify zone targets, equipment qualification, connected load, and model limitations. Export creates one JSON that can be reopened here or loaded in Telecom MTO Lite under Setup > Project > Load. Export the grid CSV separately, then print the study.
Source input

What the reference condition does

The reference condition is the known loudspeaker sound level at a stated distance and electrical power. It anchors the propagation calculation:

Lp = Lref + 10 log10(Ptap/Pref) - 20 log10(r/rref) - losses
Reference SPL
The datasheet sound level at the stated test distance and test power.
Reference distance
The distance used for that datasheet value, commonly 1 m.
Reference power
The electrical power used for that datasheet value, commonly 1 W for sensitivity.
Near-field clamp
The closest distance at which the distance-loss formula is allowed to increase level.
Tap / operating power
The transformer tap actually used by the coverage calculation and amplifier-load total.
Rated power
The loudspeaker capacity used to check the weatherproof or flameproof output requirement at 1 m.

Choose one valid datasheet convention

Sensitivity convention

If the datasheet states 105 dBA at 1 W / 1 m, enter Reference SPL 105, Reference distance 1 m, and Reference power 1 W. A 25 W tap adds about 14 dB before distance and other losses.

Rated-output convention

If the datasheet states 124 dBA at rated 25 W / 1 m, enter Reference SPL 124, Reference distance 1 m, and Reference power 25 W. Do not enter 1 W unless 124 dBA is explicitly a 1 W sensitivity value.

Avoid double-counting power

Reference SPL and Reference power must describe the same test condition. The app then adjusts that datum to the selected operating tap.

Criteria, benchmarks & model boundary

Σ
Screening model, not a ray-tracing replacement

The app applies source reference level, power adjustment, inverse-square divergence, editable horizontal and vertical directivity, fixed/air losses, simplified obstacle insertion loss, and energetic summation. It is a free-field screening model: indoor reverberant buildup can make it under-predict level beyond the critical distance, obstacle losses are binary rather than diffraction-based, and it does not calculate STI/RASTI or octave-band propagation.

Implemented calculation basis

Lp,i = Lref + 10 log₁₀(P/Pref) − 20 log₁₀(r/rref) − D − A Ltotal = 10 log₁₀(Σ 10Lp,i/10)

The 10 log power rule, 20 log distance rule, point-source divergence, 1.5 m receiver plane, and system-specific acceptance criteria come from the three IFC studies in this repository.
